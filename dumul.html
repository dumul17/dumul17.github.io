DUMUL'S OBSERVATORY

Track Notes

Glitch (Instrumental) is the static before the signal. No lyrics, just a low neural hum — DUMUL's Observatory and Limerence compressed into one long glitch, like a memory buffering and never quite loading. It opens the album already half-broken, so that everything after it sounds like a system recovering.

Limerence takes place inside the mind of someone whose love has grown heavier than the other person can carry. The narrator admits to pushing these feelings onto someone who never asked for them, apologizes, and still confesses they cannot stop. Instead of asking for understanding, the narrator invites blame, and the song ends on a half-finished sigh, an apology that never finds its ending.

Glitch is about a memory that refuses to shut down. The narrator tries to forget, but the other person's shadow keeps haunting them, and running through their own darkness leads nowhere. The chorus names the trap: with this person, they feel everything, pain included; without them, they cannot feel what love means at all. The final lines flip between "with you" and "without you" like a stuck loop, which gives the song its title.

Nastenka is heartbreak in its quietest form. The narrator accepts not being the best or most beautiful choice, tells the other person to speak honestly without worrying about hurting them, then admits in the chorus that they are crying inside. There is no anger, only disappointment, and the closing lines repeat the same realization until it sinks in: this love was never meant for them. The title recalls the heroine of Dostoevsky's White Nights, another story about loving someone whose heart belongs elsewhere.

Larung is the closing chapter: the moment of letting go. The narrator knows the other person loves someone else and tells them to stop lying to their own feelings and stop staying out of obligation. In turn, the narrator promises to stop forcing their heart and stop hoping, and asks to be forgotten. Its title refers to the tradition of setting something adrift on water, and the song plays like that kind of farewell: not a fight, but a slow release.

The four tracks of Limerence trace a single arc:

  • : threshold / intro
  • : obsession
  • : the inability to forget
  • : painful acceptance
  • : release
